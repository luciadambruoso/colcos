Our Mission: Supporting Public Health with Community Intelligence

Data are the key to improving public health in communities with deprived social circumstances. Our platform provides individuals and organisations with tools to generate new data and evidence and make informed decisions about public health services.

Data-Driven Insights for Public Health

Our project has aggregated data from different sources to provide a comprehensive picture of the cost of living and smoking in communities living with deprived social circumstances. We use state-of-the-art analytics tools to analyse data and provide actionable insights.

Versatile and User-Friendly Tools

Our tools are designed to be versatile and user-friendly. Whether you’re an individual looking for information about the cost of living and smoking in your community, or an organisation looking to conduct research, our platform has the tools you need to get the job done.

Authentic Community Empowerment

Our team of expert community researchers build new knowledge through systematised personal histories of tobacco and smoking products. Expertise is grounded in experience. We work with people directly impacted by smoking and tobacco consumption, and the costs of smoking. We work with people most excluded from planning and action to address the issue.

Published research

The study is published, peer reviewed and open access.

Cost of Living/Cost of Smoking: A Demonstration Study of Cooperative Action Learning to Understand and Address Smoking in Deprived Communities Within the Cost-of-Living Crisis

D’Ambruoso L, Stephen A, Cowan E, Innocent W, Marathia E, Littlejohn C, Duffy S, and the Cost of Living/Cost of Smoking Learning Partnership

Social Policy & Administration, 2024. doi:10.1111/spol.13082

Nine community-based participants took part in eight workshops as co-researchers, collecting and arranging new data and evidence. Three further workshops with service providers analysed and interpreted that data, appraised local action, and reflected on the process. Community partners identified a convergence between increased stress owing to the crisis and the increased availability, affordability and acceptability of tobacco-related products, particularly e-cigarettes — compounded by a lack of awareness of available cessation services.
