About the study

Community intelligence for data-driven decision-making

Smoking in deprived communities is not a lifestyle choice made in isolation. It is produced by circumstances — and the evidence systems that plan services rarely capture those circumstances. This study set out to change what counts as evidence, and who gets to produce it.

We believe that evidence generated with communities, not only about them, is the route to improving public health. Our research provides individuals and organisations with the tools they need to generate new data and evidence, and to make informed decisions about smoking cessation and prevention in their own community.

From analysing the impact of smoking on public health to understanding the cost of living in different neighbourhoods, our tools and processes build both data and dialogue — the two things needed together to realise change.

The project is based in North-East Scotland, delivered by the University of Aberdeen in partnership with ASH Scotland, NHS Grampian, and Turning Point Scotland.

The research question

What are the needs of excluded, vulnerable and at-risk populations around smoking cessation and prevention in the context of the cost-of-living crisis?

We answered it through a participatory learning process, combining community-generated intelligence with statistical and administrative data to inform and support health systems responses.

Methodologically, the research contributes to developing processes of engagement and dialogue between service users and providers, addressing health concerns from a pro-social perspective. We develop processes to engage communities and generate community intelligence on smoking as a social problem, then facilitate engagements with health systems actors to analyse and interpret that data collectively, in action-learning spaces mediated by researchers.

Objectives

  • Engage tobacco consumers and those directly impacted by tobacco consumption in deprived communities to generate new evidence on the social circumstances and drivers of smoking, cessation and prevention in the cost-of-living crisis — and document the forms, processes and contexts of that engagement.
  • Engage health systems actors in analysis and interpretation of the evidence generated, using deliberative and dialogue processes connecting service users and providers — and document the forms, processes and contexts of that engagement.
  • Promote participatory and peer learning approaches in routine health systems functions to enable collective capabilities, including by disseminating substantive and methodological findings to the public, health systems stakeholders, and governmental, technical and research groups.

The team

A collaborative of social and data scientists, service providers, health officials, policy advocates and community experts
