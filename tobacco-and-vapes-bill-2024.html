‘What about the freedom from smoking?’

As the Tobacco and Vapes Bill cleared the House of Commons by 383 votes to 67, The Press and Journal turned to the study for what people in affected communities actually think about a generational ban.

The article led with Avril Bruce, a Banff woman who started smoking at thirteen, reached forty a day, and described money for cigarettes coming before the rent. She had taken part in research with ASH Scotland. Her verdict on the Bill was unambiguous.

Lucia D’Ambruoso put the study’s finding into the national argument: that the case against such legislation is usually made as a libertarian one, the freedom to smoke — and that the study showed the other side of that coin. Rural and under-served communities most affected by the availability of these products and by their health consequences did not want them, in any form. The question is the freedom from smoking, for those communities and for the children coming after them.

Sheila Duffy of ASH Scotland made the point alongside: participants wished they had never been in the position of finding tobacco so addictive, so expensive, and so much part of their lives.
